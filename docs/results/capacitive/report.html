Sequential RMS–EMT integration

230 kV weak grid · ±100 MVAr averaged STATCOM · 150 ms three-phase fault

Engineering checks: PASS. Real PSS/E power-flow/ASCC and PSCAD/EMTDC outputs were used. Dynamic RMS comparison: Native PSS/E SVSMO3T2; matched terminal controls with documented model differences.

Method and scope

PSS/E solves the operating point and extracts the external-grid positive-sequence Thévenin impedance. Python reconstructs the source behind this impedance, converts bases and transfers parameters into a newly generated PSCAD case. PSCAD then runs independently. This is sequential, offline integration, without shared time stepping, real-time synchronization or feedback to PSS/E.

The STATCOM is an averaged reactive current injection model. Its voltage droop is 0.05 pu, current response time constant 0.02 s, and maximum phase RMS current 0.251022 kA. ±100 MVAr is the nominal-voltage capability; Q falls with voltage at the current limit. A 10 ms rotating-frame voltage filter and an ideal fixed-frequency reference are used. The model excludes PWM, DC-link energy, PLL synchronization dynamics, transformer/converter losses, protection and manufacturer controls. Filtered-angle tracking permits transient active-power exchange without a DC-energy constraint. It is a terminal surrogate and cannot establish hardware performance.

The local load is converted to parallel R, L and C branches matching its solved pre-fault net P/Q. A configurable 100 nF per-phase PCC capacitance represents an assumed aggregate terminal capacitance; it is not inferred from PSS/E or measured equipment. The parallel inductor is adjusted to preserve net Q. This finite capacitance regularizes the fast clearing transient. This is a constant-impedance EMT load; its fault response is not a constant-power load response. The fault is three simultaneous phase-to-ground resistances of 1 Ω, applied from 1 to 1.15 s and cleared without line tripping.

Measurements and comparison

EMT voltage and current are trailing one-cycle total RMS values; P is the cycle mean of Σvi and Q is the cycle mean of the balanced Clarke-frame reactive-power expression. These are appropriate for this balanced averaged model. They do not constitute a general fundamental-sequence estimator for distorted switching waveforms. The first incomplete cycle is omitted. PSS/E power flow is compared only over 0.8–0.95 s; startup and fault transients are excluded.

Model mapping and remaining differences

  • Native SVSMO3T2: Kp=1/droop, Ki=0, Xc0=0. Before RUN only, move Iq0 into Vref=Vop+droop*Iq0 and zero the inactive integral state/derivative/memory. This preserves the initial command and avoids the native limiter resetting the bias.
  • 10 ms scalar voltage filter; 20 ms output lag; +/-1 pu current limit on the same rating/base.
  • GENCLS H=0 holds internal source voltage and frequency; source plus branch impedance matches the extracted Z1.
  • Local passive load converted to constant admittance, equal to the EMT parallel RLC at nominal frequency.
  • Same PCC fault, resistance in ohms and requested application/clearing times; actual event times recorded.
  • MSS, slow reset, deadband and nonlinear droop disabled; protection thresholds lie outside this run range.
  • RMS scalar voltage and instantaneous phasor orientation differ from EMT filtered dq voltage and angle hold.
  • RMS Q is derived from simultaneous native network FLOW1 Q plus the constant-admittance load Q; the model Q VAR can lag voltage at events and is retained separately in the CSV/OUTX.
  • Native duplicate-time event samples are retained in native_channels.csv for discontinuous cycle integration. Final partial steps align fault boundaries within 0.2 microseconds; the nominal step is then restored.
  • RMS algebraic network has no subcycle inductive/capacitive energy transient; EMT retains RLC energy.
  • RMS is initialized at the operating point; EMT energization/enable at startup is excluded from comparisons.
  • Neither model includes switching, DC energy, detailed PLL or equipment protection; equivalence is bounded to terminal controls.
